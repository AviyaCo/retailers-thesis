% (1) Replaces the paragraph "Learning-rate and radius selection" of Protocol A (Section sec:protocol_validation).
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Learning-rate and radius selection.} For each experiment, sample size, and seed, the nominal policy
is trained for each candidate learning rate, and the learning rate with the smallest mean validation cost is
retained, with ties resolved in favor of the smaller value. Neural CCG is then run for every radius in the
grid, including $\rho=0$. Radii whose returned policies have a mean validation cost within $10^{-6}$ (absolute)
of the smallest one are treated as tied, and the largest tied radius is selected. Ties arise when the CCG
stopping criterion is already met by the nominal policy at several small radii: these runs then return the
same policy and attain identical validation costs. Among radii that the validation data cannot distinguish,
the rule therefore reports the largest radius at which the returned policy passed the robustness check, in the
spirit of the one-standard-error rule, which selects the most regularized among statistically
indistinguishable models~\cite{Breiman1984CART,Hastie2009ESL}. All ties observed in our experiments are exact,
and the smallest nonzero gap between the best and second-best validation cost of any reported selection is
$1.6\times10^{-3}$, so the tolerance only guards against floating-point differences and never merges distinct
scores. The selected policy is not retrained. This tie rule replaced an earlier rule that resolved ties in
favor of the smaller radius; it was adopted after the test results had been examined, but it changes no
out-of-sample cost, because every affected seed selects the identical policy (in Experiments~1 and~3, the
selection of $21$ of the $60$ seed configurations moves from $\rho=0$ to $\rho=0.03$; no selection changes in
Experiment~2 or for SRO-FA). Because the same validation observations select both the learning rate and the
radius, the validation cost of the selected policy is optimistically biased; the methods are therefore
compared only on the test set.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% (2) Replaces, in the SRO-FA benchmark description ("Protocol A."), the sentence about its radius selection.
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\emph{Protocol A.} SRO-FA is trained on exactly the same training observations as Neural CCG, and its radius
is selected for each seed on the same validation observations by the same rule (the largest radius within
$10^{-6}$ of the smallest mean validation cost); no SRO-FA validation costs tie, so every SRO-FA selection
is the unique minimizer.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% (3) Replaces the sentence before Table tab:moreau_cv_time that begins "The robust neural times correspond ...".
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The robust neural times correspond to each seed's own validation-selected radius; they do not represent one
common fixed radius. When the radii $0$ to $0.03$ tie, the selected run is the one at $\rho=0.03$, which returns
the nominal policy after an adversarial verification pass, and its recorded time is that of this run.
